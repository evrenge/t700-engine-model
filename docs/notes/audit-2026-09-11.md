# How much of this model actually agrees with Ballin?

Prompted by a fair challenge -- that the running commentary had drifted toward explaining
differences rather than closing them, and that "everything is different". This is the
census, with every deviation attributed to a named cause or flagged unexplained.

## The distribution, 318 comparable numbers

| band | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| exact zero match | 90 | 28 % |
| < 1 % | 76 | 24 % |
| < 5 % | 54 | 17 % |
| < 15 % | 66 | 21 % |
| < 50 % | 17 | 5 % |
| >= 50 % | 15 | 4.7 % |

**69.2 % within 5 %, 89.9 % within 15 %.** All nine Table B.1 trim values are under 1 %.

Twelve of the fifteen worst are the *same element* -- `A(NP,NP)`, once per trim per DOF
variant. That is one missing term counted twelve times, not twelve faults. Excluding it,
three of 318 elements are 50 % or worse.

## Attribution

| cause | elements | worst |
|---|---|---|
| `f7` knot interpolation (#43) | 50 | 62.4 % |
| tau1/tau2 (#31) | 24 | 34.9 % |
| `f1` knot interpolation (#43) | 24 | 33.6 % |
| `j_load` (#6) | 45 | 24.5 % |
| tau1/tau2 compounded with `f1` | 6 | 99.4 % |
| `dQreq/dNP` (#6) | 12 | 54.5 % |
| **unexplained** | **45** | **14.6 %** |

Only **seven elements of 318** are both unexplained and worse than 5 %. The two largest,
`A(NG,P3)` in B8 and B12 at 12.7 % and 14.6 %, are a difference of two large numbers --
Ballin's 6-DOF value is -141.9 against a 5-DOF -692.8, so the T41 path carries -551 of it
and the residue is what is being compared. Ill-conditioned, not anomalous.

## Why these are diagnoses and not stories

An explanation earns trust by predicting something it was not fitted to. These did:

* **`j_load`** was derived from `b(NP)` **alone**, then correctly predicted the other four
  NP-row elements -- from ~900 % error to within 12 %. Those four were not used to find it.
* **Knot proximity** predicted that hover would be *simultaneously* the best `f1` case and
  the worst `f7` case, because it sits mid-segment on one table and 0.0002 from a knot on
  the other. It is both.
* **The Chen derivation** predicted `G2` zero except the T41 row, `C = [I]`, and 6-DOF
  `A(2,3) = 0` where the 5-DOF prints -41.28. All three are what the report prints.
* **The T41-row diagnosis** predicted `A(T41,NG)` alone would break the row's uniform
  scaling, because it is the one element built through the `f1`-damaged NG column. It does.

The strongest single fact remains that **nine independent Table B.1 equilibrium values
close to under 1 %** with the zero structure exact across 90 elements. A model with a
structural error does not do that.

## What is honestly still open, and what cannot be closed

**`dQreq/dNP` is supplied, and supplying it proves nothing.** It takes those twelve
elements from 54.5 % to 0.0 %, but the value was recovered from those very elements. It is
a missing *input*, not a fitted parameter -- completing the load specification the way
`j_load` did -- and it makes the remaining residual visible instead of masked. It is not
evidence of agreement and is not counted as such.

**The interpolation scheme cannot be closed from the report.** It never states one, and
neither candidate fits: a shape-preserving cubic on `f7` is near-exact at level and worse
at both other trims, and a larger extraction perturbation needs a different step at each
trim because the knot distances differ. See `derivative-ambiguity.md`.

**`f_hs` versus `TC_T41` cannot be separated either -- tested, 2026-09-11.** All three
Appendix B trims sit on `f_hs`'s flat top, so a 14 % error in either is the same
correction there. Figure 10 looked like the escape: its step-down drives NGc to 71 %,
where `f_hs` has fallen to 4.35 and the two hypotheses differ by a factor of 1.73. Running
it, they do **not** separate -- T41 excursion -268.5 against -261.5, about 1.5 % apart,
far below our 11 % error on that panel. The reason is structural: the step fires at NGc
91 %, the fast response happens while still on the plateau, and NGc only reaches 71 % well
after T41 has settled. **Figure 10 never exercises the region that would distinguish
them.** No other condition in the report goes below 85 % NGc at a trim.

So two of the three open mechanisms are blocked by what the report does not print, and
that is a property of the source, not of the replication.

# Addendum, same day: the check that had never been run

The census above has a flaw its own author should have stated: **90 of the 318 "agreements"
are zero against zero.** Structural zeros test the equation topology and are worth
something, but they cannot disagree. Strip them and it is 130 of 228 within 5 %, **57 %**,
not 69 %.

Worse, every comparison in it -- Table B.1, Table 1, all twelve Appendix B matrices --
evaluates the model at **the same three operating points**. Three points cannot tell a
model that is right from a model that is right *there*.

Figures 6, 7 and 8 are Ballin's own real-time model swept across the full fuel-flow range.
They had been digitized since Phase 1 and **no test had ever touched them**.

## What the sweep found

| figure | what it tests | result |
|---|---|---|
| Figure 8, Ps3 vs %NG | compressor + station-3 volume + bleeds, **no fuel flow at all** | 24 points, worst **1.6 %**, mean -0.4 % |
| Figure 7, shp vs Wf | the whole gas path to output power | 12 of 14 within 5 %; +11.3 % at 224 lb/hr decaying to -0.8 % by 700 |
| Figure 6, %NG vs Wf | fuel flow to gas generator speed | systematic **-1.38 %NG** across 136-800 lb/hr |

Figure 8 is the most valuable of the three and it is the one that had been sitting unused
the longest: because no fuel flow appears in it, a compensating error in the combustor
cannot rescue it. It agrees over the whole range.

Figure 6 is the one we do not track, and it is the report disagreeing with itself. At the
three flows open question #46 already measured, Table B.1 sits -1.50 / -1.18 / -0.83 %NG
below Figure 6, and our sweep gives -1.55 / -1.07 / -0.83. We are on Table B.1 -- printed
numbers -- to 0.014 / 0.132 / 0.026 %, and inherit its entire quarrel with Figure 6. What
is new is that the quarrel is a **whole-curve** systematic narrowing with power, not the
three samples #46 recorded.

## And one error of mine, for the record

The first run of this sweep reported the model unable to trim above 750 lb/hr, covering
only three quarters of Figure 6. That was the analysis script cold-starting every trim
instead of using continuation. With a proper ladder the model reaches **800 lb/hr and
99.87 %NG**, and fails only at Figure 6's last point, 100.31 %NG, which is past `f1`'s
100 % top speed line -- where refusing to extrapolate is correct behaviour.

Two lessons, and the second is the one that matters: a test harness can manufacture a
defect as easily as it can hide one, and **a suspicion is worth more than a census**. The
census was accurate and complacent; the thing it could not see was the question it never
asked.
