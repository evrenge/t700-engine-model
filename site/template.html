What was built

scope and structure

Ballin had to run a component-level thermodynamic engine model in real time on 1988 hardware, at a time step of at most 10 ms [pdf p.38], while a pilot flew a helicopter around it. The replication reproduces both the model and the compromises that made it real-time: where the report is dated or arguably wrong, it is reproduced as written and the objection recorded.

Engine states
NG, NP, P3, P41, P45 — two spool speeds and three control-volume pressures
Control states
22 continuous states plus 4 memory elements (three hysteresis bands and a transport delay)
Gas path
inlet → compressor → three bleed paths → combustor → gas generator turbine → free power turbine → exhaust
Frame rate
7 ms engine, multirate 2:1 against a 14 ms rotor step — the report’s own configuration
Integration
fixed-step explicit Euler. The report never names its integrator; this is a decision, not a reading
Units
the report’s own — lbm, psia, °R, hp, ft·lbf, rpm

Why the pressures are algebraic and not integrated

The 5-state model Appendix B linearizes cannot be integrated at the report’s frame time. Table 1 puts its fastest mode at −4900 /sec [pdf p.31], so explicit integration is stable only below about 0.4 ms; the report runs at 7 ms. Ballin therefore makes the three pressures algebraic and solves them within the frame: an opened compressor mass-flow iteration that carries one flow across the frame boundary (Eq. 74), a Gauss–Seidel sweep for P3 and P41 (Eqs. 76, 78), and an independent solve for P45 (Eq. 80). Only NG and NP, at least three orders of magnitude slower, are integrated.

That is what the Conclusions mean by “The modeling of high-speed dynamics which represent changes of mass flow between the internal control volumes was found to be unnecessary” [pdf p.54]: those dynamics are the fast modes.

Two features of that line

Shaft power goes negative below {{rj:engine_steady.shp_zero_crossing_ng|.2f}} %NG. At very low fuel the free turbine absorbs rather than delivers, and the report eliminates fuel control below flight-idle power [pdf p.38]. It is also why the SFC panel starts at 50 hp.

The fuel control system

Appendix C — 22 block diagrams, 8 schedules, no equations

Appendix C specifies the complete T700 fuel control and contains no equations: one constants table, twenty-two block diagrams and eight function plots. Every signal path was traced off the page raster at 300–600 dpi, summing junction by summing junction, because a misread sign inverts the control and still produces plausible numbers.

HMU — hydromechanical unit

The primary loop. Governs gas generator speed on a droop line, applies the idle, acceleration and deceleration limits, and meters fuel. It runs alone if the electrical trim is null. Figures C9–C22.

ECU — electrical control unit

Governs power turbine speed and limits T4.5. Its entire authority is one voltage, SPDG, driving a torque motor that trims the HMU’s droop line downward. Figures C1–C8.

HMU droop line [Fig. C9, signs read at 600 dpi] WFPDM = WFPTP + KNDRP·(NGREF − PCNGHL) − WFPRF − max(DWFPL + TMRU, 0) │ │ │ │ │ │ │ └─ collective feedforward + ECU trim │ │ └─────── power lever │ └─────────────────────── speed droop └─ topping line, the ceiling. Everything else SUBTRACTS. Limit arbitration [Fig. C18] is a fixed cascade, not a min-max soup: HMUSEL = MAX( MIN( MAX(WFPDM, WFIDM), WFPAC ), WFPDC ) └ idle floor └ accel └ decel floor outranks accel Everything above is a fuel-flow PARAMETER, Wf/Ps3. It becomes a fuel flow only at the metering valve: WFMV = HMUSEL × PS3L.

The report’s closed-loop figures need the Gen Hel helicopter simulation (§8), so there is no closed-loop trace to overlay. The loop settles to a cycle, not a point

The NG speed sensor’s printed 0.05 %NG hysteresis band sustains a limit cycle: NP oscillates {{rj:closed_loop.rows.1.cycle_ptp|.1f}} to {{rj:closed_loop.rows.11.cycle_ptp|.1f}} rpm peak to peak at the three trims and does not decay. Every closed-loop number quoted here is therefore a mean over whole cycles; a terminal sample would depend on where the run happens to stop.

Source data and digitization

nineteen figures, none of which exists as numbers

The report supplies its function tables only as printed plots: eleven engine functions and eight control schedules, plus five body figures that carry the validation references. Recovering them is a measurement, and it is treated as one.

The extraction pipeline, and what each step defends against
StepMethod The failure it prevents
RenderThe figure pages are single 300 dpi 1‑bit images, so extraction works on the embedded bitmap itself (pdfimages). Inventing sub-pixel detail the scan never had.
CalibrateFrame detection, a tick lattice, then an axis polynomial through the interior ticks. Never by eye. An error large enough to move every value and small enough to look plausible in the CSV.
RectifyPage skew and keystone are a coordinate transform on the measured points; the pixels are never resampled. Interpolation touching the data. A residual bow in the ink survives, which is how f6’s apparent slope was caught.
ExtractSingle curves: a generative model fitted to each printed glyph. Multi-curve figures: column-by-column tracing, curves identified by rank because they never cross. Blob detection fails when markers sit on the joining line.
VerifyAn overlay per figure, an independent marker count, and held-out checks: Figure A2’s markers are printed at the integers 1–20, and the extraction, never told so, recovers them. Errors invisible in the CSV and obvious in the overlay.
GateEvery digitizer reruns on demand and diffs against the committed file; a failed tool fails the verdict. A gate that passes when every digitizer has crashed.

Physical conditioning, and the last column

Some of these functions have physics that must hold exactly: a bleed fraction that reaches zero must be zero, a monotone ratio must not wobble. Conditioning enforces that on load. The last column is how far it moved any value, bounded at a few times that map’s own read error (test_maps.CONDITIONING_TOL_SIGMA): past that the fix belongs at the figure. The CSVs are never edited; they carry the ink as measured.

The read error is measured, not estimated

Figures 9 and 10 each contain one panel whose true value is printed: the fuel flow is the input, with both levels in the caption. Digitizing it measures the read error directly. It is a fixed fraction of each panel’s span, not of the value — a pixel offset — which is why the transient tolerances are a percent of full scale.

Modelling decisions and departures

each one recorded, each one costed

Where the report is silent, or reproducing it literally produces something demonstrably wrong, a decision was needed. Each is listed with what it costs: a departure nobody can measure will be undone by accident.

DecisionWhat the report says What we do, and what it costs
Station 4.1 heat sink Eqs. 48–53 Prints both configurations and says which figures use which. Eqs. 48–49 are the metal energy balance; Eq. 50 is the lead–lag they collapse into. A switch, so either published configuration runs. Built on Eqs. 48–49, not Eq. 50: the collapse holds only for constant coefficients, and Eqs. 51 and 53 vary with T41 and W41, so a lead–lag memory goes stale on a fuel step.
The two heat-sink time constants τ₁, τ₂ in Eq. 63 Not printed anywhere, and trim-dependent by definition. Recovered from Appendix B’s own 3‑DOF blocks. They imply a TC_T41 larger than the printed 0.29 [pdf p.55] (test_heat_sink_taus.IMPLIED_TC_T41): Appendix B was generated with a different constant. We keep the printed one, which is why our 6‑DOF fuel column runs low (§6.3).
Opened compressor iteration Eq. 74 Reads two ways: the printed equation lags only the bleed; the prose lags the whole flow leaving the compressor. Both selectable; the prose reading ships. They converge to the same trim and differ only while a transient is under way, by a few tenths of a percent of speed at most.
Power turbine pressure solve Eq. 80 “…because it is a nonlinear function of its own value, iterative techniques must be used” [pdf p.37]. The printed fixed-point iteration runs first. Where the mass-flow table’s elasticity is below −1, most of a chop, it does not converge, and the same equation is solved exactly instead: with u = Ps9/P45 it is a piecewise-linear function against a line through the origin, so the root is unique and one divide away.
Compressor map interpolation f1, Figure A1 Seven markers per speed line and no interpolation scheme stated. A departure, still open (#60). Interpolated at constant beta, the fraction of each speed line’s own pressure-ratio span, rather than at constant abscissa, which on the Figure 10 chop reads the 65 % line far past its last knot. Against the printed table it wins on some printed surfaces and loses on others, so the scores do not decide it. Kept because a scheme that invents extrapolation is wrong whatever it scores.
Combustor efficiency f6, Figure A6 A curve with a visible slope, and no numeric table. A departure, and immaterial. Loaded as a constant: the digitized glyphs fit a horizontal line well inside the ink width, and the page’s frame edges sit at four different angles, so the slope was the paper. The digitized slope would leave every printed comparison unchanged to four significant figures.
Limited integrators Figs. C3, C7, C10 All three draw an unlimited 1/s followed by a separate saturation block — a topology that winds up. A departure, closed by decision (#59). We clamp the state, which does not wind up. The two agree on every surface the report prints and separate only under a sustained load dump, where the drawn windup overspeeds NP beyond anything printed.
Jacobian perturbation step “plus or minus two percent of equilibruim [sic] conditions” [pdf p.28] — an amplitude, matched to the rotor speed deviations of real helicopters. The report’s ±2 %, adopted (#56): a finite secant, which is what Ballin’s matrices are, and better than an infinitesimal step on Table 1 and Appendix B. The cost: Eq. 18’s square root makes the secant differ from the tangent in the pressure equations, so tests about local slopes use the tangent.
Hysteresis band convention Three control blocks carry backlash and each names a constant. None says whether it is the whole band or the half band. The total band, closed by decision (#54). Reading it the other way moves the settled state very little and no conclusion turns on it, though it changes the limit-cycle amplitude of §3.

One bias that must not be fixed

Ballin’s model underestimates turbine temperature at trim in every validation case, and he says so. Reproducing that bias with the same sign is correct; matching the hardware better would be a worse replication. The same holds for his other admitted limits — the 100 %NG ceiling, direction-independent heat-sink constants, no T4.5 heat sink, damping slightly high, load-demand compensation weaker than the real aircraft.

Real-time performance

far inside the budget, no accuracy given up

The report’s frame was at most 10 ms [pdf p.38]. The replication is not a real-time deliverable, but frame cost governs how much validation can be run. On a laptop an engine frame costs tens of microseconds, two orders of magnitude inside that budget, and the closed loop with both control units stays well inside it too. Most of the time was in the function-table lookups, not the iterations. Every change below was held to no new dependency and no accuracy loss.

ChangeHow accuracy was held
A scalar path for table lookup Every call site asks for one abscissa. The scalar path reproduces NumPy’s interpolation rather than approximating it: checked at every knot, every midpoint, random interior points and outside both ends of every shipped curve, with zero bit-mismatches.
Loop invariants out of the Gauss–Seidel sweep The report counts “eleven arithmetic operations required for each pass” [pdf p.37], which leaves no room to recompute the two invariants every pass. Hoisted, with the printed expressions’ association preserved.
Eq. 80 solved exactly The one change that is not bit-identical. It acts only where the printed iteration fails to converge (§5), and there it moves the result far below any tolerance.

Limits and what is not claimed

the boundaries are in the source

Figures 11–15 run the engine inside the Gen Hel UH‑60A blade-element helicopter simulation, which the report consumes and does not contain. They are out of scope by construction: the rotor model is an external supplier to the report exactly as it is to us.

Table 3 looks like the best steady-state benchmark, but its model column used the NASA‑Lewis prototype engine’s compressor and turbine functions, printed nowhere, and its own caption says it does not represent specification T700 performance: evidence of method, never pass/fail.

Two Gen Hel derivatives — the load-torque slope and the load inertia — are recovered from Appendix B itself rather than modelled, so the power turbine row of every linear comparison is supplied rather than predicted.

Above 100 %NG nothing is claimed. That ceiling is Ballin’s, stated in his own validation section, and a replication inherits it.
